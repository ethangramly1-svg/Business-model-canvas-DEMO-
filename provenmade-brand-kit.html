Color system

5 colors, each doing one job
Neutral

Warm ivory / charcoal ink

#FAF6F0 · #2B2622

A warm, paper-like neutral instead of stark white — signals "materials and craft," not generic e-commerce. Charcoal instead of pure black keeps body text easier on the eyes at length.

Brand / action

Clay

#C1673B

Warm, high-contrast CTA colors (orange/red family) consistently outperform cool ones in conversion testing — one 2026 study found orange buttons beating blue by 3.1 points. Clay keeps that warm-CTA advantage while reading as "handmade," not "discount site."

Trust / verification

Verified green

#2F6B4F

Green checkmarks measurably move trust — one study found a simple green check raised perceived trustworthiness by 37% versus an identical listing without one. Reserved only for the Proof Score and verification states, so it never loses meaning.

Pending

Amber

#B98A2E

For a seller still building their Proof Score. Deliberately not red — an incomplete score should read as "in progress," not "failed" or punished.

System error only

Reserved red

#B3413A

Held back for genuine failures — a declined payment, a broken upload. Never used for an unverified or low-score listing, so red keeps its urgency instead of becoming background noise.

Why no blue, even though research says blue wins at checkout: the standard advice is a blue accent for payment trust. Provenmade skips it on purpose — green is already the brand's one trust signal, tied directly to the Proof Score. Carrying that same green through checkout reinforces "this is the verified marketplace" instead of borrowing the generic bank-blue every other checkout uses. Contrast still needs to hit at least 7:1 for text against its background at every step — that consistently matters more to legibility than which hue is chosen.
