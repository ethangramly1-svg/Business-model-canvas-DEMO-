# Online Business Idea Research
*Compiled August 17, 2026 — grounded MVP scope, ranked by evidence strength*

Ten researched, currently-underserved online business ideas, followed by a full deep-dive concept and a ready-to-use wireframe prompt for the strongest pick.

---

## The Top 10, Ranked

### 1. Verified-Human-Made Marketplace — ★ Very Strong ★ (this is the deep dive below)
Etsy is actively bleeding trust right now: active buyers fell 3.4% to 86.5M in 2025 and general-merchandise sales dropped 6.8% YoY as AI-generated and dropshipped goods flooded listings, and long-time sellers are visibly migrating off the platform ([Neura Market](https://www.neura.market/news/etsy-sellers-flee-ai-goods-policy-complaints), [Winzheng](https://www.winzheng.com/en/article/etsy-flop-era-sellers-ai-knockoffs)). A rival, Fybe, just launched banning AI/dropshipping — but enforces it with volunteer moderators, which won't scale. The global handicrafts market is $739.95B (2024) heading to $983.12B by 2030 ([Grand View Research, via Alibaba](https://www.alibaba.com/product-insights/top-5-handmade-marketplaces-in-2026-where-artisans-thrive.html)). Nobody yet combines cryptographic, camera-level proof of authorship (the same C2PA standard Leica/Sony/Nikon/Canon now ship and OpenAI uses to sign AI output) with a per-listing trust score. That's the open lane.

### 2. Micro-Retirement / Career-Break Planning Platform — ★ Very Strong ★
Demand for paid micro-retirement and sabbatical planning is projected to go from $0.55B in 2026 to $11.5B by 2036 — a 35.5% CAGR, the fastest of anything researched here ([Fact.MR](https://www.factmr.com/report/micro-retirement-and-sabbatical-planning-services-market)). Gen Z sabbatical rates jumped from 1.7% to 8% in four years ([Benefit News](https://www.benefitnews.com/news/micro-retirements-are-trending-among-gen-z-workers)), and the market is explicitly described as "moving from informal break planning toward paid advice" — with no dominant platform named anywhere in current coverage.

### 3. Cross-Marketplace Secondhand-Luxury Verification Layer — ★ Strong ★
41% of secondhand-luxury buyers worry about authenticity and 36% remain unsure even after using an authentication service; 61% of European buyers specifically want a digital certificate ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/10/18/secondhand-luxury-soars-authentication-becomes-a-new-gold-standard.html), [Legitique](https://legitique.com/blogs/case-studies/global-counterfeit-luxury-report-2026)). The market itself ($13.47B → $22.13B by 2031) is already contested by vertically-integrated players (The RealReal, Vestiaire Collective, Entrupy), so the wedge isn't another marketplace — it's a neutral, marketplace-agnostic verification layer/browser companion that works across all of them.

### 4. All-in-One Aging-Parent Care Coordination Hub — ★ Strong ★
The elderly-care-apps market is $8.7B in 2026, heading to $34.13B by 2035 (16.4% CAGR), and the core complaint is structural: families juggle four or five separate apps for meds, providers, records, and family updates because no single tool covers all of it ([Fact.MR / MarkWide Research](https://markwideresearch.com/elderly-care-apps-market)). Real pain, real market — but more crowded already (CircleCare, Neela, Caring Village), so the win condition is consolidation plus employer-benefit-channel distribution rather than a novel mechanism.

### 5. Warranty & Home-Maintenance Autopilot — ★ Moderate-Strong ★
Centriq, the decade-old home-inventory app, shut down for good on January 31, 2025, wiping out its users' data ([Medium](https://medium.com/@justlivejustbe/centriq-shut-down-what-do-i-do-now-a41f26295caf)). Several trackers (Dib, HomeBeacon, HomeZada) rushed in to catch the refugees, but all of them still just *track* warranties — none *auto-files* the claim when something breaks. That's the differentiator left on the table.

### 6. Cross-Border Tax Autopilot for Freelancers & Digital Nomads — ★ Moderate-Strong ★
Real, recurring pain: FEIE limits, VAT/OSS/IOSS filings, and treaty complexity across jurisdictions ([Taxes for Expats](https://www.taxesforexpats.com/articles/expat-tax-rules/digital-nomad-taxes.html)). Existing tools (Xolo, etc.) are mostly "form a company with us first" products; a lighter-weight tracking/filing layer that works regardless of entity structure is the gap.

### 7. Vertical AI Voice Receptionist for Home-Service SMBs — ★ Strong ★
For HVAC, pest control, and roofing companies, a single-niche AI phone agent can reach $10K MRR with no sales team, sold through trade-association channels ([Preuve.ai](https://preuve.ai/blog/emerging-ai-startup-ideas-2026)). Scrappy, solo-founder-buildable, and distribution is already solved via trade associations.

### 8. Time-Blindness-First Planning App for Self-Employed Neurodivergent Workers — ★ Moderate ★
ADHD/neurodivergent productivity is a real, underserved category — body-doubling, time-blindness accommodation, and sensory-friendly design are still largely unaddressed by mainstream tools ([NicheMetric](https://www.nichemetric.com/blog/underserved-app-markets-2026)). Tiimo (2025 Apple App of the Year) already owns the broad "neurodivergent planner" space, so the honest wedge is a narrower one: freelancers/self-employed people specifically, where invoicing and client deadlines intersect with time blindness in ways general planners don't address.

### 9. AI Disruption-Prediction for Mid-Market Manufacturers — ★ Moderate-Strong ★ (B2B)
The AI-in-supply-chain market is $13.81B in 2026, but the $10M–$500M-revenue manufacturer segment is described as having "almost no funded competition" at accessible price points (~$2K/month) ([Preuve.ai](https://preuve.ai/blog/emerging-ai-startup-ideas-2026)). Longer sales cycle, but a real, quantified whitespace.

### 10. AI-Native EU AI Act Compliance Tooling for SMEs — ★ Moderate ★ (B2B)
A forced-buying event (regulatory deadlines) rather than organic demand, which makes it a reliable but less "delightful app" business — included for completeness since it's one of the most evidence-backed 2026 gaps in the research.

## Deep Dive: Idea #1 — A Verified-Human-Made Marketplace

### Working name
**Provenmade** (alternates: Handproof, TrueCraft, Verihand)

### The problem, in one line
Buyers on handmade marketplaces can no longer tell what's actually made by a person — and the incumbent (Etsy) is losing both sellers and buyers over it, in real time, right now.

### Why now
1. Etsy's June 2025 Creativity Standards update and inconsistent enforcement triggered a visible seller exodus and a documented drop in active buyers ([Neura Market](https://www.neura.market/news/etsy-sellers-flee-ai-goods-policy-complaints)).
2. A new direct competitor (Fybe) just proved the demand for "AI-free" is real — but it relies on volunteer human moderators, which is a ceiling, not a moat.
3. Camera and AI-industry infrastructure now exists to make cryptographic authorship verification practical for a small team to build on top of, rather than invent from scratch: the C2PA standard is already shipping in Leica, Sony, Nikon, and Canon firmware, and OpenAI signs its own AI output (Sora, DALL·E) with Content Credentials + SynthID watermarking ([Internet Pros](https://internet-pros.com/blog/ai-content-provenance-watermarking-c2pa-2026/), [StockTitan/Rekor](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/REKR/rekor-introduces-go-secure-video-proof-that-video-is-0v4248vwue2t.html)). Provenmade flips the same infrastructure around: instead of proving "this was made by AI," it proves "this was made by a human, over time, by hand."

### The unique mechanism (the actual moat)
Every other player in this space verifies trust in a way that doesn't scale or doesn't travel with the item:

| Competitor | How they build trust | Why it's not enough |
|---|---|---|
| Etsy | Seller self-disclosure checkbox | Weakly enforced, easy to fake |
| Fybe | Volunteer human moderators | Doesn't scale past a small catalog |
| Nest's Verified Handcrafted™ | Org-level audit/certification mark | A badge for the *seller*, not proof for *each item* |
| Goimagine | Self-reported "Craft Journey Map" | Self-reported, not independently verified |

Provenmade's mechanism: **mandatory in-app process capture, cryptographically timestamped, AI-cross-validated, and attached to the individual listing** — not the seller profile.

- **Capture**: sellers record 3–6 short, in-app video/photo checkpoints during the actual making process (not just a finished-product photo). Capture happens inside the Provenmade app so timestamp, device ID, and geolocation are signed at the moment of recording (C2PA-style manifest).
- **Cross-validation**: a lightweight AI pass checks internal consistency across the checkpoints — tool marks, material state progression, hand/lighting continuity, elapsed real time between frames — to catch staged, reused, or stock footage. This is a fraud-detection filter, not a creativity judge.
- **Proof Score**: each listing gets a visible, buyer-facing score (e.g., "Verified Process — 5/5 checkpoints, cryptographically signed") with a tap-through timeline buyers can actually scrub through, the way they'd check a Carfax before buying a used car.
- **Portability**: because the record is a signed manifest, not a platform-locked badge, sellers can show the same proof if they ever migrate marketplaces — which also makes it easy to poach frustrated Etsy sellers, since they lose nothing by trying Provenmade in parallel.

### Target user
- **Sellers (supply side)**: independent makers in jewelry, woodworking, ceramics, and textiles currently on Etsy who are frustrated by AI/dropship flooding and inconsistent enforcement — a large, currently-agitated, easy-to-find population (active in Etsy seller forums and Reddit threads right now).
- **Buyers (demand side)**: gift-givers and collectors willing to pay a premium specifically *because* it's verifiably handmade — the same psychographic driving the 61%-want-certificates stat in secondhand luxury.

### Business model
- Marketplace take rate: 5% + payment processing (undercutting Etsy's combined ~6.5%+fees) to make switching costs low for sellers.
- **Provenmade Pro** subscription for top sellers: lower take rate, priority search placement, bulk process-capture tools.
- Buyer-side "Authenticity Guarantee": small insurance-backed add-on at checkout (refund if a Proof Score is later invalidated) — a monetizable trust feature none of the competitors offer.

### MVP scope (deliberately grounded)
- Web app + mobile-responsive (skip native iOS/Android for v1 — cheaper, faster).
- Launch with 3 categories only: jewelry, woodworking, ceramics (highest trust-sensitivity, easiest to visually verify process).
- Core loop: list → capture process (guided in-app flow) → AI consistency check → Proof Score assigned → buyer browses/searches → checkout with escrow.
- Go-to-market: recruit directly from the visible Etsy-exodus conversation (seller forums, Reddit, the Nest Verified Handcrafted™ program as a credibility partner) rather than cold-market advertising.

### Real risks (stated honestly)
- Cold-start problem shared by every marketplace — mitigated by the current, unusually large pool of already-frustrated Etsy sellers to recruit from.
- AI consistency-checking will have false positives/negatives early on — needs a human appeals path at launch, not full automation.
- A well-funded incumbent (Etsy itself, or Nest) could copy the mechanism — the defensibility is being first to make the *portable, per-item, cryptographic* version, not the general idea of "verification."

## Wireframe / Design Prompt

Copy everything in the block below into an AI design tool (v0.dev, Galileo AI, Uizard, Figma's First Draft, etc.) or hand it to a designer as a creative brief.

```
PRODUCT: Provenmade — a marketplace for cryptographically verified, human-made goods.
ONE-LINER: "Etsy, but every listing carries provable proof a human actually made it."

POSITIONING & MOOD:
Warm and tactile like a craft marketplace, but with the visual confidence of a
fintech-grade trust product. Think "artisan meets verification badge" — not sterile
tech, not twee craft-fair. The verification UI (scores, timelines, certificates)
should feel as credible as a boarding pass or a Carfax report, sitting inside a
warm, materials-forward marketplace shell.

TARGET USERS:
1. Buyers: gift-givers and collectors who will pay a premium for verified authenticity.
2. Sellers: independent makers (jewelry, woodworking, ceramics) migrating off Etsy.

COLOR PALETTE:
- Base neutrals: warm off-white (#FAF6F0), charcoal ink (#2B2622)
- Craft accent: terracotta/clay (#C1673B) for primary actions and brand moments
- Trust/verification accent: a distinct cool green (#2F6B4F) reserved ONLY for
  verification badges, Proof Scores, and signed/checkmark states — never used
  decoratively, so it stays meaningful
- Alert/unverified state: muted amber (#B98A2E), never red (avoid feeling punitive
  toward sellers still building their Proof Score)

TYPOGRAPHY:
- Headlines: a warm serif (e.g., a humanist serif like Tiempos or similar) for
  brand/editorial moments — product titles, seller stories
- UI/body: a clean, highly legible grotesk sans (e.g., Inter or similar) for
  scores, timelines, prices, and all functional UI
- Numerals in the Proof Score and timeline should use tabular/monospaced figures
  so they feel precise and auditable

KEY SCREENS TO DESIGN (desktop + mobile):

1. HOMEPAGE / DISCOVER
   - Hero: a single verified item with its Proof Score visible, not generic lifestyle
     photography — leads with trust, not aesthetics alone
   - Category rails (Jewelry / Woodworking / Ceramics) with a small "X% verified
     process" stat per category
   - A short explainer strip: "How verification works" (3-step icon row: Capture →
     Cross-check → Proof Score)

2. LISTING PAGE
   - Standard product gallery, BUT the first "image" in the gallery is the process
     timeline, not a glamour shot — a horizontal scrubber/filmstrip of the 3–6
     timestamped checkpoints, each with a small signed/timestamp icon
   - A prominent Proof Score badge near the price (e.g., a circular 5/5 badge with
     "Cryptographically signed" microcopy and a tap-to-expand certificate)
   - Standard price, variants, add-to-cart below the fold of the trust elements —
     trust comes first, commerce second

3. VERIFICATION CERTIFICATE (modal or full page, reachable by tapping the Proof Score)
   - Looks like a boarding-pass / Carfax-style certificate: item name, maker,
     capture timestamps, device-signed manifest ID, a visual checklist of what
     was verified (tool-mark consistency, elapsed time, lighting continuity)
   - A small "What this does NOT mean" disclosure line for honesty (verifies
     process, not artistic quality)

4. SELLER: PROCESS CAPTURE FLOW (the core differentiating interaction)
   - A guided, camera-first, step-by-step in-app capture flow (NOT a file upload)
   - Each step shows a progress tracker (Checkpoint 1 of 5), a live timestamp
     overlay, and simple in-context guidance ("Capture the raw material before
     you start")
   - After capture: a processing state ("Verifying consistency...") before the
     Proof Score is assigned — make this feel like real computation happening,
     not instant/fake

5. SELLER DASHBOARD
   - Listings table with Proof Score column, take-rate shown transparently,
     and a comparison callout vs. Etsy's fees to reinforce the switching pitch
   - A "Portable Proof" export action (download/share your signed manifests) —
     visually reinforce that this data belongs to the seller, not the platform

6. BUYER SEARCH & FILTER
   - Standard marketplace filters (category, price, material) PLUS a unique
     "Minimum Proof Score" filter/slider — make verification a first-class
     filter axis, not an afterthought

7. CHECKOUT
   - Standard cart/payment flow, with an optional "Authenticity Guarantee" add-on
     line item (small insurance-backed upsell) clearly explained in one line

LAYOUT/GRID:
- 12-column responsive grid, generous whitespace, warm neutral backgrounds —
  avoid dense e-commerce grids; this should feel closer to an editorial/craft
  publication than a discount marketplace

ACCESSIBILITY:
- Verification states must never rely on color alone (pair the green/amber
  states with icons + text labels)
- Certificate/timeline content must be fully readable by screen readers,
  including timestamp and manifest data

DELIVERABLE FORMAT:
Produce both desktop (1440px) and mobile (390px) frames for screens 1–4 at
minimum, using the palette, type, and component notes above.
```
