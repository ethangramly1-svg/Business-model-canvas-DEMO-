Provenmade — design theme

How the site should feel and behave, backed by current e-commerce and marketplace research — plus the name, slogan, and imagery decisions that came out of it.

Company name

Keeping "Provenmade."

It does the job a marketplace name needs to do: it's short, it reads instantly as a compound of "proven" + "made," and it directly names the mechanism (proof of making) instead of a vague craft-adjacent word. No reason found to change it.

A direct search for provenmade.com turned up no existing site or registration — a good sign, not a guarantee. Worth confirming through an actual registrar (Namecheap, GoDaddy, etc.) before treating it as secured.

Slogan

pick one, or mix and match
Recommended Every listing, verifiably made. Already doing double duty as the hero headline — using it as the slogan too keeps one consistent line instead of two competing ones. States the mechanism, not a feeling.
Proof, not promises. Shorter and punchier; leans into the "Etsy is full of fake handmade claims" contrast without naming a competitor.
See it made. Trust it's real. Most literal — describes the actual capture flow. Best used near the seller-capture screen rather than as the primary tagline.
The process is the proof. Most abstract of the four; reads well on a certificate or About page, less well as a first-impression headline.

Navigation principles

research-backed
Sticky header, always reachable

A fixed header keeps search, categories, and cart reachable without scrolling back up — a common pattern on marketplace sites, now applied to the demo's home screen.

Navigate by how people shop, not by internal categories

Shoppers think "jewelry," "wood," "ceramics" — not SKU or supplier codes. Category tiles now double as real filters instead of static decoration.

69% of shoppers rely on search, but most still leave unsatisfied

A working search box is no longer optional polish — it's the primary way most visitors find a specific item. Added a live search to the demo header.

Source: Nosto — "Why 69% of Shoppers Use Search, but 80% Still Leave"
Breadcrumbs on every product page

Reduces disorientation once someone is a few clicks deep. Already present on the listing screen; kept as-is.

Baymard Institute finds 58–67% of e-commerce sites have mediocre-to-poor navigation. Cheap to get right, expensive to skip. Source: Baymard Institute

Imagery & photography style

what the research says, applied

2026 handmade-marketplace trends lean toward personalization (custom pieces "that can't be found in a regular store"), materials with a real story (recycled and upcycled materials, not just raw ones), and pages built as immersive experiences that tell a brand's story rather than plain catalogs. Provenmade's whole mechanism — verified process footage — is already a stronger version of that "tell the story" idea than most marketplaces attempt, so this is more validation than a required style change.

  • Real photography, not icon-style illustrations — swapped the placeholder line-art for licensed stock photos (Pexels, free for commercial use) across all 9 products.
  • Product-forward shots preferred over lifestyle/model shots — closer to how a real listing photo reads, and avoids implying a stock model is "the seller."
  • Photo quality is one of the biggest levers in the research — 75% of online shoppers say it's "very" or "extremely" important to their purchase decision, which is why this pass prioritized replacing every placeholder before anything else.
Sources: The Bespoke Markets — 2026 Handmade & Artisan Business Trends, Retail Technology Review

Color — confirmed, not changed

checked against the real photos

Pulled the dominant tones out of the product photography now in use — coppers, warm woods, unglazed stoneware, sage greens — and they sit comfortably inside the palette from the brand kit. No changes needed; the warm-neutral base with clay and verified-green accents already reads as "materials and craft," which is the goal.
